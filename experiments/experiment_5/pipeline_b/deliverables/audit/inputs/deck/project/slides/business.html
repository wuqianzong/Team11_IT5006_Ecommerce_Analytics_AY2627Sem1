Business problem

Growth stalled when delivery broke; two models act before the damage

Growth stalled

Revenue 2.5× from Mar 2017 to Apr 2018, but no month since topped Nov 2017 (R$ 1,010,016)

Service broke

Late deliveries 5.3% in Oct 2017, 21.4% in Mar 2018; detractors 11.9% to 22.5%

Retention low

Over 14 months only 2.9% of customers ordered more than once; within a month at most 2.3%

Goal: restart sales growth and lift retention. Problem: promise a delivery date Olist can keep, and reach unhappy customers before they write the review.

Observation: 57.2% of late orders got a 1–2 star review, against 9.5% of orders on time.

Lead-time model, at checkout → sales

A shorter promise the operation keeps. Shorter promises raised sales in an experiment and a simulation (Cui et al., 2024; Salari et al., 2022): a literature-supported hypothesis for Olist.

Detractor alert, at handover → retention

Customer service reaches at-risk customers before the review. That contact improves reviews or repeat orders is an unmeasured mechanism, to be tested in a pilot.

0:30. Why does this matter? Olist's revenue grew 2.5 times, then stalled after the November 2017 peak. At the same time delivery broke: late deliveries rose to 21.4% in March 2018 and detractors to 22.5%, and late orders get bad reviews far more often. Over fourteen months only 2.9% of customers ordered more than once, so one bad delivery is often the whole relationship. So the goal is to restart sales growth and lift retention, and our problem is to promise a date Olist can keep and reach unhappy customers before they review. The lead-time model serves the promise, the alert serves customer service.

## 9 Business recommendations

The two models serve one chain of decisions about an order: the promise at checkout, the seller's dispatch, the carrier's transit and, when the order is still late, service recovery before the review. Each step is labelled as an observation (measured here), a literature-supported hypothesis, an unmeasured mechanism, a scenario or an operational rule. The test-period numbers in this section are a post-test analysis of Pipeline B's {{BC.test_orders}} test orders, computed from the stored predictions after the test was read, with no model refitted; the buffer of {{B12.reg.buffer_90}} days chosen on validation remains the unbiased result. Table K16 sets the actions side by side.

**1. Promise: a shorter date the operation keeps** (observation; literature-supported hypothesis for sales). Olist's own estimated delivery date is long and loose: on the test orders it promised a median of {{BC.promise.olist_median}} days, missed the actual lead time by {{BC.promise.olist_mae}} days on average, and {{BC.promise.olist_late}} of orders arrived after it. Pipeline B is far closer (MAE {{BC.promise.B_mae}} days) but, shown as a date, it is late for {{BC.promise.B_late}} of orders, as any prediction near the middle of the outcomes must be. A buffer turns the prediction into a promise, and its size is a business dial (Table 5).

At about Olist's late share, Pipeline B plus 6 days promises {{BC.promise.B6_median}} days instead of {{BC.promise.olist_median}} ({{BC.promise.B6_late}} late against {{BC.promise.olist_late}}); at Olist's promise length, plus 10 days is late for only {{BC.promise.B10_late}}. The 12-day buffer is conservative because validation includes the late-2017 crisis, when Pipeline B without a buffer was late for {{BC.buffer.all_validation_share_late_no_buffer}} of orders, against {{BC.promise.B_late}} on the test. We therefore recommend an operational rule: each month, set the buffer on the most recent weeks, as the smallest buffer whose late share is at or below that of Olist's own estimate on those weeks. On the last validation fold ({{BC.buffer.recentolist_first_purchase}} to {{BC.buffer.recentolist_last_purchase}}, where Olist's estimate was late for {{BC.buffer.recentolist_olist_validation_share_late}}) the rule gives {{BC.buffer.recentolist_buffer_days}} days, chosen without test data; a 10% target on the same weeks would give {{BC.buffer.recent10_buffer_days}} days ({{BC.buffer.recent10_test_share_late_B}} late on the test). That shorter promises which are kept help sales is a literature-supported hypothesis for Olist: faster actual delivery raised online sales at a US apparel retailer (Fisher et al., 2019), and promising from a delivery-time forecast raised sales in a simulation on JD.com data (Salari et al., 2022). Promising too fast has costs after the sale (Cui et al., 2024), which is why the dial is tied to the late share.

**2. Dispatch: the seller is the lever** (observation). Under the recommended promise, {{BC.expedite.B10_late_with_late_handover}} of the {{BC.expedite.B10_late_orders}} late orders ({{BC.expedite.B10_share_of_late_with_late_handover}}) were handed to the carrier after the seller's shipping limit. Orders whose shipping limit passes without handover are detractors with precision {{B9.clf.missed_dispatch_precision}}, and a rule that flags them catches {{B9.clf.missed_dispatch_share}} of all detractors on the test period, with no model. The seller's earlier dispatch delay and the route's recent speed are among the strongest predictors (section 7.4); that working with slow sellers would cut late deliveries is an unmeasured mechanism, because nothing here tests an intervention. The dashboard ranks routes and categories by observed outcomes for each quarter so seller management can choose where to look first.

**3. Transit: expediting after a late handover saves few orders** (scenario; unmeasured mechanism). If the carrier's transit had been 10% faster for the late orders handed over late (the dashboard's "logistics 10% faster" scenario, applied to the actual transit time), {{BC.expedite.B10_on_time_if_transit_10pct_faster}} of those {{BC.expedite.B10_late_with_late_handover}} orders would have arrived on time, {{BC.expedite.B10_share_of_all_late_on_time}} of all late orders; against Olist's own estimate, {{BC.expedite.olist_on_time_if_transit_10pct_faster}} of {{BC.expedite.olist_late_with_late_handover}}. Whether a carrier can deliver such a speed-up, and at what cost, is not measured. Most late orders therefore need service recovery.

**4. Service recovery: alert at handover** (observation for volumes and precision; operational rule for the workflow; unmeasured mechanism for the effect of a contact). Pipeline B at 4/15 sends {{B10.clf.B_4_15_test_alerts_day}} alerts a day at precision {{B10.clf.B_4_15_test_precision}}, so customer service reaches about {{BC.reach.B415_reached_day}} real detractors a day (reach = precision × alerts a day). 4/15 is a capacity choice; under the 5:1 weighting the cost-optimal threshold is 1/6. The baseline's fallback at 1/6 adds {{BC.reach.A16_alerts_day}} alerts a day at precision {{BC.reach.A16_precision}}, below the missed-dispatch rule's {{B9.clf.missed_dispatch_precision}}; with it the total is {{B10.clf.combined_test_alerts_day}} a day at precision {{B10.clf.combined_test_precision}}. Two lighter options are open to the business: the fallback at 4/15 ({{BC.reach.A415_alerts_day}} a day at precision {{BC.reach.A415_precision}}, a post-test view of a threshold not tuned on the test), or the missed-dispatch rule alone outside the seven states (Table 6).

**Workflow** (operational rule, not a measured result). In the dashboard, customer service sets the threshold on the detractor tab, then works the ML deployment tab: the daily queue (4.1) lists Pipeline B's alerts first, by risk; the baseline fallback's alerts come next, by handover delay, longest first, then by order value. The case file (4.2) shows what is known at handover; the agent contacts the customer with a tracking update and offers a voucher for the next order. Missed dispatch is flagged when the shipping limit passes. The shortlist (4.3) and the routes with repeated late handovers go to seller management each week, and the promise buffer and alert precision by state are reviewed each month. The review cap of {{B10.clf.review_cap}} alerts a day applies to Pipeline B alone; the team accepted the higher combined volume.

**Freight discount** (association, not an effect). Among the orders still late under the recommended promise after the transit scenario, Pipeline B flags {{BC.discount.B10_flagged}} ({{BC.discount.B10_detractors}} of them detractors). Cutting freight by 30% moves {{BC.discount.B10_f30_stops}} of them below 4/15 and by 50% {{BC.discount.B10_f50_stops}}; free shipping moves {{BC.discount.B10_free_stops}}, but all of those ({{BC.discount.B10_free_outside}}) fall below the lowest freight the model was trained on, so the result is an extrapolation. These are changes in the model's predicted risk, not the effect of a discount on the review.

**Cost and break-even** (assumption). Weighting a missed detractor as five false alerts, the serving rule's test cost is {{B10.clf.combined_test_cost_5_1}} ({{B10.clf.B_4_15_test_cost_5_1}} from Pipeline B and {{B10.clf.A_out_test_cost_5_1}} from the fallback). A contact pays when precision × the value kept per detractor reached exceeds the cost of a contact; neither value is known here, so no figure is given. A randomised hold-back pilot would measure it (section 10).

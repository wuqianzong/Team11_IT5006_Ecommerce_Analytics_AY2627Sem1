## 9 Business recommendations

The models serve one chain: promise, dispatch, transit and, if the order is still late, service recovery before the review. This section reuses the saved test predictions and labels of Pipeline B's {{BC.test_orders}} test orders after the test, with no refit; the {{B12.reg.buffer_90}}-day buffer chosen on validation remains the unbiased result. Tables K16 and K18 to K22 give the detail.

**1. Promise** (observation; literature-supported hypothesis for sales). Olist's own estimate promised a median of {{BC.promise.olist_median}} days, missed the actual lead time by {{BC.promise.olist_mae}} days on average, and was late for {{BC.promise.olist_late}} of orders; Pipeline B shown as a date is late for {{BC.promise.B_late}}. A buffer turns the prediction into a promise, a business dial (figure below, Table K18). Candidate rule: the smallest buffer with at most {{BC.buffer.recent5_target_share_late}} late on the most recent weeks; on the last validation fold, without test data, it gives {{BC.buffer.recent5_buffer_days}} days, which on the test promises a median of {{BC.promise.B6h_median}} days with {{BC.promise.B6h_late}} late: shorter than Olist's promise and late less often (matching Olist's late share on those weeks, {{BC.buffer.recentolist_olist_validation_share_late}}, would need {{BC.buffer.recentolist_buffer_days}} days). It was set on one window and needs a rolling backtest before monthly use. The 12-day buffer is conservative because validation includes the late-2017 crisis, when Pipeline B without a buffer was late for {{BC.buffer.all_validation_share_late_no_buffer}} of orders, against {{BC.promise.B_late}} on the test. Shorter promises raised sales in an experiment (Cui et al., 2024) and a simulation (Salari et al., 2022), but lowered retention in the experiment, so the dial is watched against reviews.

**2. Dispatch and transit** (observation; scenario). Under that promise, {{BC.expedite.B6h_share_of_late_with_late_handover}} of late orders left the seller after the shipping limit. A rule that flags missed dispatch has precision {{B9.clf.missed_dispatch_precision}} and catches {{B9.clf.missed_dispatch_share}} of all detractors in the seven states, with no model. Had transit been 10% faster for those late handovers, only {{BC.expedite.B6h_share_of_all_late_on_time}} of late orders would have arrived on time, so most need service recovery; that working with slow sellers cuts late deliveries is an unmeasured mechanism.

**3. Service recovery** (observation). Pipeline B at 4/15 sends {{B10.clf.B_4_15_test_alerts_day}} alerts a day at precision {{B10.clf.B_4_15_test_precision}}, reaching about {{BC.reach.B415_reached_day}} detractors a day. The fallback at 1/6 adds {{BC.reach.A16_alerts_day}} a day at precision {{BC.reach.A16_precision}}, for a total of {{B10.clf.combined_test_alerts_day}} a day at precision {{B10.clf.combined_test_precision}} (populations in Table K19). 1/6 is the cost threshold set before the test, cheaper than no alert on validation for both models; 4/15 is a capacity choice. On the test the fallback's alerts cost more than none ({{BC.cost.test_A16}} against {{BC.cost.test_A_none}}; Table K21), so a higher fallback threshold is an option to test. The missed-dispatch rule serves the seven states beside Pipeline B, not the fallback's orders.

**Proposed workflow** (operational rule; the dashboard covers the queue, the contact needs integration and the pilot). Customer service would set the threshold, work the daily queue (Pipeline B by risk, then the fallback by handover delay and order value), read each case file and let the reason for the risk pick the action (Table K22), from a new arrival date and an apology to a freight refund or a voucher; the contact happens outside the dashboard, and each week the priorities go to seller management. The review cap of {{B10.clf.review_cap}} alerts a day applies to Pipeline B alone. A freight discount moves few still-late alerts below 4/15 ({{BC.discount.B6h_f30_stops}} of {{BC.discount.B6h_flagged}} at 30% off; Table K20), an association, not an effect.

**Cost** (assumption). Weighting a missed detractor as five false alerts, the serving rule's test cost is {{B10.clf.combined_test_cost_5_1}} ({{B10.clf.B_4_15_test_cost_5_1}} from Pipeline B, {{B10.clf.A_out_test_cost_5_1}} from the fallback), against {{BC.cost.test_serve_none}} with no alerts. A contact pays when precision × the value kept per detractor reached exceeds its cost; both values are unknown, so a randomised hold-back pilot comes first (section 10).

Problem: promise a delivery date Olist can keep, and reach unhappy customers before they write the review.

Observation: {{A3.clf.detractor_rate_late}} of late orders got a 1–2 star review, against {{A3.clf.detractor_rate_on_time}} of orders on time.

Lead-time model, at checkout → sales

A shorter promise the operation keeps. Shorter promises raised sales in an experiment and a simulation (Cui et al., 2024; Salari et al., 2022): a literature-supported hypothesis for Olist.

Detractor alert, at handover → retention

Customer service reaches at-risk customers before the review. That contact improves reviews or repeat orders is an unmeasured mechanism, to be tested in a pilot.

0:30. Why does this matter? Olist's revenue grew {{BIZ.rev_growth}} times, then stalled after the November 2017 peak. At the same time delivery broke: late deliveries rose to {{A3.trend.late_2018_03}} in March 2018 and detractors to {{A3.trend.detractor_2018_03}}, and late orders get bad reviews far more often. Over fourteen months only {{BC.retention.share}} of customers ordered more than once, so one bad delivery is often the whole relationship. So our problem: promise a date Olist can keep, and reach unhappy customers before they review. The lead-time model serves the promise, the alert serves customer service.
